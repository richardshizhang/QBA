    How this was made

    Everything here comes from what faculty have published, not from what anyone reported about their own work.

    Papers

    Each person on the QCB faculty page was matched by hand to their records in OpenAlex, a free catalog of scholarly papers. Peer reviews, corrections and supplementary files were dropped, along with papers by other people who share their name. That left papers.

    Research similarity

    A language model trained on scientific papers (SPECTER2) turned each paper's title and abstract into a list of 768 numbers, placing similar papers close together. A person's position is the average of their papers. Recent papers count more, as do papers where they're first or last author.

    The map

    t-SNE and UMAP squeeze those 768 dimensions into two. Both keep each person near their closest research neighbors, at the cost of distances between distant groups, which don't mean much. The two views are different summaries of the same data, so trust what they agree on.

    Clusters

    Average-linkage hierarchical clustering on the same numbers; the Tree view shows the full hierarchy. The cluster names were written by hand.

    Lines

    Two people are joined if they share a paper. Each shared paper adds 1 ÷ (authors − 1) to the pair's strength, so a two-author paper counts 1 and a 100-author consortium paper about 0.01.

    Finding your match

    The model above is too big to run in your browser, so each of about 7,700 research terms comes with a precomputed score for every faculty member, from how much they use the term and how close the papers using it are to theirs. Your words are looked up and averaged, so nothing you type leaves your browser. Given only the title of a paper it hadn't seen, this finds the real author in the top 5 about 88% of the time, but your own words will be vaguer than a title. Your dot sits at the weighted average of your closest matches, which is an estimate: t-SNE and UMAP have no exact way to add a new point. A thrown dot works in reverse, blending the people nearest where it lands.

    What it can't tell you

    It only sees published work, so new collaborations and unpublished projects won't appear, and some papers will be missing or misattributed. It says nothing about who is taking students. Check with faculty directly.
